How Shell and BP Use IoT and AI to Cut Operational Costs

2 June 2026 5 min read

The Energy Sector's Digital Revolution

The oil and gas industry has long been characterized by high operational costs, complex infrastructure, and challenging working conditions. Today, companies like Shell, BP, and Saudi Aramco are fundamentally changing how they operate by integrating Internet of Things (IoT) technology and artificial intelligence (AI) into their core operations. The results? Substantial cost savings, improved safety, and enhanced productivity across their global operations.

This digital transformation isn't just about staying competitive—it's about survival in an increasingly volatile energy market where margins are thin and efficiency is paramount. By deploying thousands of connected sensors and intelligent analytics platforms, energy giants are achieving what seemed impossible just a decade ago: real-time operational visibility and predictive maintenance at scale.

Real-World Examples: Shell's Digital Excellence

Shell has emerged as a pioneer in applying IoT and AI to reduce operational costs. The company deployed the Shell GameChanger program, which invests heavily in digital innovations. One notable implementation involves using IoT sensors across their offshore platforms to monitor equipment health continuously.

By collecting real-time data from thousands of sensors positioned throughout their facilities, Shell's AI systems can predict equipment failures before they occur. This shift from reactive maintenance to predictive maintenance has generated millions in annual savings by preventing costly emergency repairs and unplanned downtime. When equipment fails unexpectedly on an offshore platform, the cost can exceed hundreds of thousands of dollars per day in lost production alone.

Shell also leverages machine learning algorithms to optimize energy consumption across their refineries. These systems analyze operational patterns and automatically adjust processes to minimize waste and reduce electricity costs—a significant line item in refinery budgets.

BP's AI-Powered Operations Centers

BP has taken a different but equally impressive approach. The company established digital operations centers that serve as nerve centers for their global operations. These centers use AI to process massive volumes of data streaming from wells, pipelines, and processing facilities worldwide.

One key innovation involves using computer vision and machine learning to monitor pipeline integrity. Instead of relying solely on periodic manual inspections—which are expensive and time-consuming—BP's AI systems analyze thermal imaging and other sensor data to identify potential corrosion or structural issues early. This capability has reduced unplanned maintenance events and extended equipment lifespan significantly.

BP also implemented predictive analytics for workforce optimization. By analyzing historical data on equipment performance and maintenance patterns, their AI systems forecast staffing needs more accurately, reducing unnecessary overtime and deployment costs.

Key Technologies Driving Cost Reduction

Several specific technologies are delivering these impressive results:

  • Condition-Based Monitoring: IoT sensors continuously track equipment conditions (temperature, vibration, pressure) and transmit data to cloud platforms where AI algorithms analyze patterns and alert operators to anomalies before failures occur.
  • Predictive Maintenance Algorithms: Machine learning models trained on historical maintenance data can predict component failures with surprising accuracy, sometimes 30-60 days in advance.
  • Process Optimization: AI systems analyze thousands of process variables simultaneously to identify opportunities for efficiency improvements that human operators might miss.
  • Asset Management Systems: Integrated IoT platforms provide complete visibility into equipment location, condition, and performance, enabling better resource allocation and utilization.
  • Data Analytics Dashboards: Real-time visualization tools help operators quickly identify issues and make informed decisions without relying on manual reporting.

Quantifying the Financial Impact

The financial benefits are substantial and well-documented. Industry research indicates that companies implementing IoT and AI solutions in oil and gas operations achieve:

  • 15-25% reduction in maintenance costs through predictive capabilities
  • 10-20% improvement in asset utilization and uptime
  • 5-15% reduction in energy consumption through process optimization
  • 20-30% faster issue resolution and emergency response
  • Significant safety improvements, reducing costly incidents and regulatory penalties

For a major oil company with global operations, these percentage improvements translate into hundreds of millions of dollars in annual savings.

Practical Takeaways for Your Business

While most businesses aren't operating offshore oil platforms, the underlying principles apply across industries. Consider these actionable insights:

  • Start with Your Biggest Cost Drivers: Identify where equipment downtime or inefficiency creates the most financial impact, then focus your IoT and AI investments there first.
  • Invest in Data Infrastructure: You can't analyze what you don't measure. Begin by deploying sensors and establishing systems to collect quality operational data.
  • Build Your Analytics Capabilities: Data alone provides no value. Develop or partner with experts who can transform raw data into actionable insights.
  • Prioritize Integration: IoT and AI solutions must integrate with your existing systems. Siloed data and tools create friction and limit value.
  • Start Small and Scale: Don't attempt company-wide transformation overnight. Pilot projects in specific departments or facilities provide proof-of-concept and valuable learning before enterprise-wide rollout.
  • Focus on Maintenance and Operations First: These areas typically offer the highest ROI for IoT and AI investments.

Overcoming Implementation Challenges

Energy companies have faced real challenges in implementing these solutions—legacy systems, complex infrastructure, and significant technical expertise requirements. However, the emergence of cloud-based platforms, managed AI services, and industry-specific solutions has made implementation more accessible even for mid-sized enterprises.

The key is partnering with technology providers who understand both your industry and your existing infrastructure, ensuring smooth integration and maximum value realization.

The Path Forward

The oil and gas industry's embrace of IoT and AI demonstrates how digital transformation delivers tangible business value. These aren't theoretical technologies—they're actively reducing costs, improving safety, and enhancing competitiveness for the world's largest energy companies.

Whether your business operates in energy, manufacturing, logistics, or utilities, similar principles apply. Connected sensors, intelligent analytics, and predictive capabilities can transform your operations and bottom line.
