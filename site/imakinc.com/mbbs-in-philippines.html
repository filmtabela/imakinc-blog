Overview

Why MBBS in Philippines?

The Philippines is one of the most popular destinations for Indian students pursuing MBBS abroad. With NMC-approved universities, English as the medium of instruction, and a US-pattern curriculum, it offers a genuinely world-class medical education at a cost far below Indian private medical colleges.

Filipino medical universities follow the American medical education system — students complete a 2-year BS Biology (pre-med) followed by a 4-year Doctor of Medicine (MD) programme. The total duration including internship is approximately 5.5 years. This US-pattern training significantly improves performance in FMGE (now NExT) and international licensing exams.

English is the official language of the Philippines and the sole medium of instruction in all medical colleges — Indian students face zero language barrier from day one. The large Indian student community across Manila, Cebu, and Davao makes the transition smooth.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is NEET required for MBBS in Philippines? +
Yes. As per NMC regulations, Indian students must have a valid NEET qualification score to pursue MBBS abroad, including Philippines. There is no minimum cutoff beyond qualifying marks.
Is Philippines MBBS recognised in India? +
Yes. Philippines MBBS degrees from NMC-approved universities are recognised in India. Students must clear the NExT (formerly FMGE) screening exam to practice medicine in India after returning.
What is the FMGE / NExT pass rate for Philippines students? +
Philippines has one of the higher FMGE pass rates among MBBS abroad destinations — typically 30–45% pass rates, compared to 15–20% for some other countries — owing to the English-medium US-pattern curriculum.
What is the total duration of MBBS in Philippines? +
The total duration is approximately 5.5 years — 2 years of BS Biology (pre-med) + 4 years of Doctor of Medicine (MD) + 1 year internship (can be completed in India).
Is there a language barrier in Philippines? +
No. English is an official language of Philippines and the sole medium of instruction in all medical colleges. Filipinos are among the most fluent English speakers in Asia — Indian students face no language adjustment issues.
